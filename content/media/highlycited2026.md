**Gioele La Manno has been named a Clarivate Highly Cited Researcher in the Cross-Field category for 2026**, his third consecutive inclusion following [2024](/media/highlycited2024/) and [2025](/media/highlycited2025/). His primary affiliation on the list is EPFL.

Announced on 18 November, this year's list recognises **6,802 researchers across more than 1,200 institutions in 62 countries and regions**. Clarivate awarded 7,090 recognitions, including 3,251 in the Cross-Field category. The distinction between researchers and recognitions matters: some people appear in more than one subject category. **Switzerland accounts for 151 recognitions**, placing it eighth worldwide and representing 2.1% of the total.

Selection draws on papers that rank among the most cited 1% for their field and publication year over an eleven-year period. Citation data are assessed alongside expert review and checks on publication and citation patterns. The result recognises sustained influence on subsequent research, rather than the reception of a single recent paper.

For Gioele, the Cross-Field category is a fitting reflection of a research path that connects **neuroscience, molecular biology and computational methods**. His work asks how the brain's cellular diversity emerges, while developing ways to measure and interpret that diversity. Contributions range from mapping developing brain cell types and introducing RNA velocity to investigating the spatial organisation of lipids in the brain. These approaches give other researchers tools and reference maps for asking their own biological questions.

The recognition also comes relatively early in that research journey. When Gioele first appeared on the list in 2024, [EPFL described him, then aged 34, as one of the youngest researchers on the list](https://actu.epfl.ch/news/four-epfl-professors-among-clarivate-s-highly-ci-5/). This third inclusion accompanies a year in which the lab and its collaborators published the [Lipid Brain Atlas](/post/lipid_brain_atlas/), extending the study of brain diversity beyond genes and cell types to its molecular composition.

Behind the individual name on the list is a collective effort: students, colleagues, mentors and collaborators who have developed methods, generated data and shared ideas. We are grateful to everyone who has contributed, and to the researchers who continue to use and build on this work.
